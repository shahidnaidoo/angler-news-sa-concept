THE KNOWLEDGE BEHIND THE CAST

What’s the
barometer telling you?

Pressure helps explain the weather around your session. Its effect on a bite is less straightforward: species, depth, tide, temperature and local conditions all matter.

Check your coast’s pressure trend Vinesh Soogreem · ANSA / Supplied via East Coast Radio
01Falling pressure

Watch what’s coming.

A falling barometer can accompany a low or approaching front. Wind, cloud and sea conditions may change with it. Some anglers associate the change with feeding, but pressure alone cannot promise a bite.

On an exposed KZN beach or point, check the wind direction and swell trend before deciding where to fish. A weather window is more useful than chasing one “magic” pressure number.

02Rising pressure

A higher number isn’t a calm-sea guarantee.

Pressure often rises behind a front. Along the South African coast, a ridging high can still drive strong onshore wind and rough water. Read the pressure trend alongside local wind and swell forecasts.

For rock-and-surf fishing, assess the actual water and your access route. Pressure does not tell you whether a ledge, estuary mouth or beach is safe.

03Steady pressure

Build your own local pattern.

A steady barometer describes one part of the conditions. Record tide stage, water clarity, wind, temperature and time of day alongside your catches; compare sessions at the same mark.

Fish can respond to pressure changes, but there is no established universal “perfect hPa” for South African coastal species. A small freshwater perch study found no significant pressure–feeding link; it does not establish a rule for KZN marine fish.

Read the barometer as a weather clue.
Then read the water.
